**You:** What animal has a prime number of legs?

**Legora:** The animal most commonly cited as having a prime number of legs is the insect — insects have 6 legs, and 6 is... actually not prime (it equals 2 x 3).

So let us think more carefully:

- **2 legs** — birds (and humans). 2 is a prime number.
- **3 legs** — no naturally occurring animals have exactly 3 legs.
- **5 legs** — some starfish (echinoderms) have 5 arms/legs. 5 is prime.
- **7 legs** — no standard animal, though some starfish can have 7 arms. 7 is prime.
- **11 legs** — some species of isopod (crustaceans) have 11 pairs, but not 11 individually.

The most clear-cut and well-known example is probably:

- **Starfish (sea stars)** — the common five-armed starfish has 5 appendages, and 5 is prime.

Another interesting case is crabs and insects in larval stages, but for a clean, natural answer, the five-armed starfish is the classic example of an animal with a prime number of limbs.
